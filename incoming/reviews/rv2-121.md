## Main theorems against the plan row

Row 121: Efron–Stein for independent coordinates on finite sets, `Var f ≤ ∑ᵢ E[Varᵢ f]`; so with
bounded differences `cᵢ`, `Var f ≤ ∑ cᵢ²/4`.

**`variance_le_sum_variance_coord` (lines 316 to 320): FAITHFUL.** The conclusion is
`variance p f ≤ ∑ i, expect p (coordVar p i f)`. That is the row's `Var f ≤ ∑ᵢ E[Varᵢ f]` under the
product law `weight p x = ∏ i, p i (x i)`, which is what independent coordinates means here.
`[∀ i, Fintype (Ω i)]` is "finite sets". `hp0` and `hp1` make each `p i` a probability vector,
which is the row's setting. Both are used: the proof passes them to `es_aux`, whose successor case
uses `hp1` in `variance_split` and `expect_coordVar_zero`, and `hp0` and `hp1` in `coord_succ_le`
(through `weight_nonneg` and `wvar_mix_le`).

**`variance_le_sum_sq_div_four` (lines 325 to 344): FAITHFUL.** `hc` is the row's bounded
differences: changing coordinate `i` alone, at any point `x` and between any two values `a` and
`b`, moves `f` by at most `c i`. The conclusion is `variance p f ≤ ∑ i, c i ^ 2 / 4`, which is the
row's. Every hypothesis is used. `hp0` goes to `wvar_le_of_bdd` and `weight_nonneg`. `hp1` goes to
`wvar_le_of_bdd` and `sum_weight`. `hc` gives `wvar_le_of_bdd` its band. All three go on to
`variance_le_sum_variance_coord`. No sign hypothesis on `c` is needed or present, because
`c i ≥ 0` follows from `hc` itself.

The six definitions (`wmean`, `wvar`, `weight`, `expect`, `variance`, `coordVar`, lines 59 to 82)
say what their docstrings and the module docstring say. `coordVar p i f x` does not depend on
`x i`, so `expect p (coordVar p i f)` is `E[Varᵢ f]`.
